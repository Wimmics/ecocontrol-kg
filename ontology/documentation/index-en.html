abiotic disorderc back to ToC or Class ToC

IRI: http://ns.inria.fr/ecocontrol/ontology/AbioticDisorder

An abiotic factor causes a disorder for a plant (or a plant part, possibly at a development stage).
Example 
The leaves burned/dried because of frost.
The plants are affected by leaf chlorosis, it is due to lack of sunlight.
is in domain of
cause op, disorder op, object op

afflictionc back to ToC or Class ToC

IRI: http://ns.inria.fr/ecocontrol/ontology/Affliction

An affliction is the result of a disorder or disease that affects a plant (or a plant part, possibly at a development stage) and eventually kills it or not.
Example 
Brown spot is a harmful disease caused by Alternaria alternata, a pathogenic fungus. It is moderately infectious but not lethal for potato (Solanum tuberosum).
Rose leaf affected by black spot disease.
Septoriosis affects soft wheat.
is in domain of
affects op, disorder op, has lethality dp

attacksc back to ToC or Class ToC

IRI: http://ns.inria.fr/ecocontrol/ontology/Attacks

A pest (possibly at a development stage) attacks a target plant (or a plant part, possibly at a development stage) at a period of time.
Example 
The larvae of the Eastern pine shoot borer feed on Pinus species, mainly Pinus strobus and Pinus sylvestris.
is in domain of
host plant op, periodicity op, pest op

biotic disorderc back to ToC or Class ToC

IRI: http://ns.inria.fr/ecocontrol/ontology/BioticDisorder

An animal or a microbe (possibly at a development stage) causes a disease for a host plant (or a plant part, possibly at a development stage). Constraint: either the disease or the plant must be explicitly known (both cannot be anonymous at the same time).
Example 
The citrus mite causes acoriosis of the lemon tree.
Xylella fastidiosa causes Pierce's disease of the grapevine.
is in domain of
agent op, disease op, host plant op

is alternative host plant ofc back to ToC or Class ToC

IRI: http://ns.inria.fr/ecocontrol/ontology/IsAlternativeHostPlantOf

Secondary plant that supports a pest, a pathogen or an organism during at least one stage of its life cycle, in the absence of the primary host plant. Often used during an unfavorable period (e.g. winter) or for a specific phase of the cycle (e.g. sexual reproduction for some pathogens).

is beneficial plant ofc back to ToC or Class ToC

IRI: http://ns.inria.fr/ecocontrol/ontology/IsBeneficialPlantOf

Cultivated or non-cultivated plant, intentionally introduced or encouraged or maintained, providing an agronomic or ecological benefit for a crop (called beneficiary). Examples: protecting crops from a bioaggressor, either by directly attracting the bioaggressor (trap plant) to draw it away from the crops, or by attracting auxiliary insects (predators, parasitoids, food insects of vectors, etc.).
Example 
The flowers of yarrow (Achillea millefolium) provide nectar and pollen to various auxiliary insects, such as bees or hoverflies and some parasitoids. Hoverfly larvae can then consume aphids present on the crops. Bees allow the pollination of the beneficiary crop.
has super-classes
is host plant of c
is in domain of
beneficiary op

is commensal ofc back to ToC or Class ToC

IRI: http://ns.inria.fr/ecocontrol/ontology/IsCommensalOf

An animal or microbe is a commensal of an animal. The commensal (the species that benefits from the association) may obtain from the host nutrients, shelter, support or a means of locomotion. The host is not significantly affected.
Example 
Some epiphytic bacteria, such as Pseudomonas spp., use the exudates present on the surface of the leaves of a cultivated plant as a source of nutrients and as a habitat, without significantly affecting the plant.
Vector insects are possibly hosts of commensal microbes (rare), actually more often mutualists or parasites, rather than strict commensals.
has sub-classes
is vector of c
is in domain of
commensal op, host animal op

is parasite ofc back to ToC or Class ToC

IRI: http://ns.inria.fr/ecocontrol/ontology/IsParasiteOf

An animal (possibly at a development stage) is a parasite of an animal (possibly at a development stage). Lethality is not necessary for the parasite (unlike parasitoidism), even though the host may die in some cases.
Example 
Trichogramma brassicae, a small parasitoid wasp, which parasitizes the eggs of certain pest insects, notably the corn borer (Ostrinia nubilalis).
has super-classes
is natural enemy of c
has sub-classes

is in domain of
parasite op

is parasitoid ofc back to ToC or Class ToC

IRI: http://ns.inria.fr/ecocontrol/ontology/IsParasitoidOf

An animal (possibly at a development stage) is a parasitoid of an animal (possibly at a development stage) that it inevitably kills during or at the end of its development. An insect is said to be host of its parasitoid. Likewise, a parasitoid is host of its hyperparasitoid. Parasitoidism is a special case of parasitism in conjunction with predation (but different from PreysOn, which implies a brief relation). Lethality is the norm (majority of cases). If the host does not die, this usually means the death of the parasitoid.
Example 
Ageniaspis citricola is a wasp parasite of the citrus leafminer larva, Phyllocnistis citrella.
Trichogramma wasps are released in crops to control the corn borer.
has super-classes
is natural enemy of c
has sub-classes
is hyper-parasitoid of c
is in domain of
parasitoid op

IRI: http://ns.inria.fr/ecocontrol/ontology/IsPrimaryHostPlantOf

Main cultivated plant on which a pest, a pathogen or an organism develops, reproduces or causes damage in an optimal way. Often the target crop or the most susceptible plant.
Example 
For Candidatus Phytoplasma vitis, the flavescence dorée phytoplasma, the primary host is the cultivated grapevine (Vitis vinifera).

is refuge ofc back to ToC or Class ToC

IRI: http://ns.inria.fr/ecocontrol/ontology/IsRefugeOf

A refuge plant is a non-cultivated plant, offering habitat, food (sap, alternative prey) or protection (egg-laying site) to a harmful or beneficial insect, during unfavorable conditions (cold, drought, temporary absence of the crop). The insect can be a pest, a vector (itself host of the pest or pathogen of something else), or a parasitoid of a pest.
Example 
During winter, the hazel (Corylus avellana) is a refuge for predatory mites (Phytoseiidae) of phytophagous mites such as the red mite (Panonychus ulmi); in spring, the predatory mites can colonize the grapevine (Vitis vinifera) and take part in regulation.
The psyllid Cacopsylla pruni, vector of the phytoplasma 'Candidatus Phytoplasma prunorum', leaves Prunus in summer and uses conifers such as Norway spruce (Picea abies) as overwintering hosts; in early spring, adults return to fruit trees of the genus Prunus, such as plum (Prunus domestica), where they reproduce and can transmit the phytoplasma.
has super-classes
is host plant of c
is in domain of
periodicity op

is reservoir ofc back to ToC or Class ToC

IRI: http://ns.inria.fr/ecocontrol/ontology/IsReservoirOf

A reservoir plant is a non-cultivated plant: wild, become wild (e.g. abandoned vine crop), or adventice, which harbors and maintains (often permanently or cyclically) a potential source of contamination for cultivated plants. This excludes auxiliary plants, which have a positive role and are used for it. In ecology, the term alternative host plant is rather used (more general than refuge or reservoir).
Example 
Wild, abandoned vines or infected vine regrowth (Vitis vinifera subsp. sylvestris): they can constitute reservoirs of the flavescence dorée phytoplasma Candidatus Phytoplasma vitis and harbor its vector insect, Scaphoideus titanus.
has super-classes
is host plant of c

is vector ofc back to ToC or Class ToC

IRI: http://ns.inria.fr/ecocontrol/ontology/IsVectorOf

An animal is a vector of an animal/microbe (transmitted) for a host plant. The transmitted organism may be pathogenic (but not necessarily) for the host plant. Vector is understood here in the biotic sense, not to be confused with an abiotic vector, which is a means of dissemination (of a pest or pathogen). Vector is a type of commensalism: the transmitted animal is commensal of the vector animal.
Example 
Xylella fastidiosa is a bacterium that spreads from one plant to another through sap-sucking insects, such as leafhoppers, which feed on plant sap and transmit the bacterium as they move between plants.
has super-classes
is commensal of c
is in domain of
host plant op, transmitted op, vector op

IRI: http://ns.inria.fr/ecocontrol/ontology/Manifestation

Symptoms manifest as the appearance of punctiform spots visible on both sides of the orange tree leaf.

An animal (possibly at a development stage) is a predator of an animal (possibly at a development stage). The relation is brief, unlike parasitoidism, which lasts for the duration of the parasitoid's stage.
